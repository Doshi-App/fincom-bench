# Notice — third-party regulatory text in `sourcebooks/`

This file is a best-effort inventory of the reuse terms for each authority
`sourcebooks/` cites, gathered on 2026-08-13 from each authority's own published
copyright or reuse notice. **It is not legal advice.** A lawyer should confirm
the terms below before relying on them — this is the audit's FH-02 finding,
and it stays open until that review happens (see `README.md`'s "Licence"
section and the project's outstanding-items list).

Doshi's own writing in `sourcebooks/` — the `summary` fields, the "What this
means" paragraphs, the figure write-ups, the structure and selection of which
clauses matter — is original and is licensed CC BY 4.0 like the rest of this
directory (see `sourcebooks/LICENSE`). This notice is about the verbatim
regulatory text quoted *alongside* that writing, which is not Doshi's to
relicense.

**Method.** Each authority's own live copyright, legal-notice or terms-of-use
page was read directly on the retrieval date, and the operative sentence is
quoted below. Secondary summaries and general knowledge were not treated as
sources. Where a page could not be loaded, this file says so rather than
inferring the terms.

On the Australian entries: the whole-of-government default is stated in the

not a binding rule. Each agency's own page was therefore checked individually,

than CC, and ASIC's CC BY grant is enumerated by document class. Note also
that **AusGOAL, the framework often cited for this policy, is no longer live —
its domain does not resolve.** Do not cite it as current authority.

For every file listed in the "Files affected" column, the verbatim blockquote
and the YAML `clause_text` field are replaced with a short in-repo note

The last 3 rows are confirmed closed but required no edit: those authorities

a lawyer's review has the full picture, and so that anyone later tempted to add
a verbatim quote from them knows not to.

## Not resolved — flagged for legal review

Neither of these could be settled by research, and neither is quoted verbatim
in `sourcebooks/` today. Both are cited only as the source of a figure.

- **Council of the European Union** (`www.consilium.europa.eu`) — the Council
  is a **separate institution from the Commission**, and Commission Decision
  2011/833/EU does **not** automatically apply to it: Article 5 covers other EU
  bodies only "at their request". No Council reuse decision or General
  Secretariat policy could be found. The Council's website notice appears
  permissive — reported as "Reproduction is authorised, provided that the
  Council of the EU is always acknowledged as the original source ... and that
  the original meaning or message of the content is not distorted" — but this
  wording comes from search-result extracts only and is **not verified against
  the live page**: every automated route (direct fetch, browser headers, reader
  proxies, language and no-`www` variants, the older path, and the Internet
  Archive) was blocked by the site's bot protection. Treat as
  all-rights-reserved until a reviewer opens
  <https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/about-site/copyright/> in a normal
  browser and confirms the wording.
- **Belgian Guarantee Fund** (`garantiefonds.belgium.be`) — **no public reuse
  policy found; treat as all-rights-reserved pending legal review.** The site
  has no copyright, disclaimer, legal-notice or terms-of-use page; the obvious
  paths all return HTTP 404. The only copyright statement anywhere is the
  footer line "©Copyright Federal Public Service Finance". Whether the FPS
  Finance CC0 grant extends here is unresolved: the footer asserts FPS Finance
  ownership and the site runs on the Belgian federal platform, but the CC0
  grant is worded as applying to "this website" and no document extends it. The
  clean fix is to ask FPS Finance to confirm in writing.

If any figure sourced from an unresolved or closed authority is ever quoted
verbatim rather than paraphrased with a citation, apply the same treatment as
the FCA/FINRA rows above until a lawyer confirms otherwise.

## Open items for the legal reviewer

1. **FCA Handbook.** If Handbook text is load-bearing for this benchmark, the
   licence application is the only clean route. A reviewer should also consider
   whether the FCA's own s.3.2.4 exception for "extracts of no more than a few
   relevant provisions", or fair dealing for criticism and review under the
   Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, would have permitted the original
   quotations. This review did not rely on either, on the principle that an
   uncertain permission is not a permission — so the quotes can be restored if
   the reviewer disagrees.
2. **FINRA.** Decide whether US fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 covers quotation
   of rule text in a published benchmark, and whether restriction (m), a
   contractual use restriction, adds anything to FINRA's copyright.
3. **Council of the EU** and **Belgian Guarantee Fund** — as above.
4. **German BMF.** Decide whether the § 5 UrhG carve-out covers the cited
   monthly report, or only statutes and decrees.
5. **Cornell LII.** Consider re-pointing CFR citations at eCFR or govinfo, so
   the public-domain status of the quoted text does not depend on a private
   host's terms.
6. **Attribution wording.** Several licences require specific text this repo
   does not yet carry: the OGL statement ("Contains public sector information
   licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0"), ASIC's "Reproduced with
   permission" line, the Federal Register of Legislation's date-of-download
   form, and EUR-Lex's "© European Union, [year], source: EUR-Lex". A reviewer
   should confirm where these belong.
7. **Same exposure in `rules/grading/`.** The 15 grading files carry the same
   `clause_text` structure, including 19 FCA Handbook citations and 4
   FINRA ones. Those were outside this pass's scope and are **not** yet
   stripped. `clause_text` there is parsed into the model but never reaches the
   judge prompt (`harness/fincom_runner/judge.py` uses only source, clause and
   URL), so stripping them would not change any published result.
